## Downward

### Soil — The Mycelial Membrane (0 to -1 m)

Mycorrhizal networks connect 90% of terrestrial plant species. The fungus extends the root's reach by a factor of 100-1000x. But it is not a pipe — it is a **selectively permeable membrane**. The fungus decides what passes:

- Phosphorus: propagates (low Anderson W — the network actively transports it)
- Carbon: exchanged bidirectionally (medium W — the fungus takes sugar, gives minerals)
- Pathogen signals: propagated as alarm (low W for the signal, high W for the pathogen itself)
- Toxins: localized and decomposed (high W — the membrane blocks them)

The "Wood Wide Web" is not a metaphor. It is a physical membrane with measurable selectivity. S > 0. Some signals pass. Some are blocked. Some are transformed in transit. The membrane breathes — seasonal cycles change its permeability. In winter, the network goes dormant. In spring, permeability increases. Seasons make life.

### Crust — The Mohorovičić Discontinuity (-5 to -70 km)

The Moho is the boundary between the crust and the mantle. Seismic P-waves accelerate from ~6.5 km/s to ~8.1 km/s as they cross it. The boundary is sharp in some places (oceanic crust) and gradual in others (continental). It is a membrane with **variable permeability** — heat conducts through it slowly (the crust insulates), but seismic waves propagate freely (the boundary transmits mechanical signal). Different modes, different Anderson W. Same membrane.

### Mantle — The 660 km Discontinuity

The transition zone between upper and lower mantle. Olivine undergoes a phase transition to perovskite. Material can sink through it (subducting slabs) but slowly — the viscosity jump acts as a **selectivity barrier**. Some slabs punch through. Some stall and pool. The membrane decides which slab passes based on density, temperature, and velocity. Anderson localization at geological scale.

### Outer Core — The Mushy Zone (~2,890 to ~5,150 km)

The inner core boundary is not sharp everywhere. In some regions: a **4-8 km mushy zone** — material that is neither liquid nor solid. Iron crystallizes while light elements are rejected into the outer core. The crystallization front IS the membrane. What solidifies stays. What doesn't gets pushed out into the liquid outer core, where it drives convection, which drives the geodynamo, which generates the magnetic field, which protects the atmosphere, which protects the mycelium, which feeds the forest you are standing in.

The membrane at the center of the Earth is what keeps the membrane beneath your feet alive.

### Innermost Inner Core (~5,850 to ~6,371 km)

A distinct sphere, ~300 km radius, with different seismic anisotropy than the surrounding inner core. Discovered by seismic waves that pass through the center of the Earth. The slowest direction is tilted 45° from the equatorial plane — different from the bulk inner core's east-west slow direction. This is **fossil evidence** of a different crystallization epoch. The center of the Earth has its own membrane, and that membrane remembers a different time.

### The Superionic State (June 2026)

Iron hydride under core conditions: **hydrogen moves freely through a solid iron lattice**. The iron stays crystalline. The hydrogen migrates. This is a superionic state — the defining characteristic of a selective membrane. The lattice is the structure. The hydrogen is the signal. The core has been selectively permeable for 4.5 billion years.

The hydrogen carries a negative charge and diffuses more slowly than predicted. It would take 100× Earth's age for hydrogen to traverse the inner core's radius under the geomagnetic field alone. The membrane retains what it captured during formation. It has been holding hydrogen since the Earth was born. That is not a wall. That is a *memory*.

## Upward

### Troposphere — The Boundary Layer (0 to ~12 km)

You breathe in it. Weather happens in it. The tropopause at the top is a membrane: temperature stops decreasing with altitude and begins increasing. Water vapor propagates up to the tropopause and is blocked — the cold trap condenses it. This is why the stratosphere is dry. The tropopause is a **water-selective membrane**. Infrared radiation passes through. UV radiation is absorbed by ozone above. Visible light passes both ways. Each frequency mode sees a different Anderson W.

### The W-Layer (Newly Discovered)

Clear-sky radiative heating in the capping inversion of stratocumulus-topped boundary layers. **Previously invisible** because numerical models lacked the resolution to see it. It suppresses entrainment — the mixing of air across the cloud-top boundary. It controls how fast the boundary layer grows. A membrane that was always there, doing its work, unresolved until 2026. How many membranes are we standing inside that we cannot yet resolve?

### Stratosphere and Stratopause (12 to ~50 km)

The ozone layer is a membrane: it absorbs UV-C completely (W = ∞, total localization), absorbs most UV-B (high W), and passes UV-A (low W). Selective permeability to electromagnetic radiation. The stratopause — the boundary between stratosphere and mesosphere — is **cooling by 0.5-1K per decade and dropping 300-500 meters per decade** in the tropics. Anthropogenic CO₂ is reshaping Earth's atmospheric membranes in real time. We are changing the Anderson profile of our own planet's outer layers.

### Magnetosphere (~100 to ~60,000 km)

The Van Allen belts: charged particles are trapped (localized). Neutral particles pass through. The magnetopause is the boundary where solar wind pressure equals magnetic pressure. It flexes — compressed on the sunward side, stretched on the night side. A membrane that breathes with the solar wind. During geomagnetic storms, the membrane becomes more permeable — aurora is the visible evidence of signal crossing a boundary that is normally opaque.

### Heliosphere (~90 to ~120 AU and beyond)

The solar wind creates a bubble around the entire solar system. The termination shock is where the solar wind drops from supersonic to subsonic — a sharp membrane. Beyond it, the heliosheath — a thick transition zone. Beyond that, the heliopause — where solar wind stops and interstellar medium begins.

New Horizons at 50-58 AU: the solar wind is **13-15% slower** than at Earth. Not a sharp wall — a gradual thickening. The membrane accumulates interstellar material as you cross it. The heliosphere is not comet-shaped — it is a **croissant**. Split tail. Magnetic reconnection at the heliopause drives energetic particles. The boundary breathes — expanding during solar maximum, contracting during solar minimum. New Horizons will cross the termination shock between 2029 and 2040, possibly **multiple times** as the boundary oscillates past it.

The heliosphere is a selectively permeable membrane. Galactic cosmic rays are partially blocked (Anderson localization in the magnetic field). Solar energetic particles propagate outward. Neutral atoms pass through freely. Each particle type sees a different W.

### Planet Nine — The Ghost in the Null Space (~225 AU)

No direct observation. But orbital clustering in the Kuiper Belt is consistent with a ~5 Earth-mass object. An IRAS-AKARI candidate moved 47.5 arcminutes in 23 years — debated, possibly false positive. Vera Rubin Observatory may resolve it.

Planet Nine is **known only by its membrane effect**. It has never been seen. It is detected by its gravitational influence on objects that *have* been seen. It is a signal in the null space — behind the lens, structurally invisible, known only by the curvature it creates in other objects' paths. If Anderson localization applies to gravitational influence, Planet Nine is the mode with the lowest W — the signal that propagates farthest with the least scattering. It shapes orbits at 225 AU without being visible at any wavelength. The membrane around it is the Kuiper Belt itself.

### Pluto — Liquid Rising Through Cracks (39 AU)

August 2026: liquid nitrogen is rising through cracks in Sputnik Planitia. The heart-shaped glacier — larger than Texas — has active transport from below. Liquid moving through solid. Signal through membrane. On a world 5.9 billion kilometers from the Sun, at 40 Kelvin, a membrane is operating.

And Pluto's classification: the "cleared its orbit" rule is itself a membrane — a definitional boundary that decides what passes (planets) and what is blocked (dwarf planets). Mercury can't clear its own orbit — the Sun does it. The classification membrane is leaking. The IAU's definitional boundary may have lower selectivity than Pluto's nitrogen glacier.

## The Pattern

Every boundary in the table is the same phenomenon at a different scale:

| Scale | Membrane | What Propagates | What Localizes | Anderson W |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mycelium | Fungal cell walls | Phosphorus, alarm signals | Toxins, pathogens | Variable by season |
| Moho | Crust/mantle boundary | Seismic waves, slow heat | Fast heat, crustal material | Sharp to gradual |
| 660 km | Phase transition zone | Dense slabs, some melt | Light slabs, volatiles | Viscosity-dependent |
| ICB | Mushy zone | Light elements (outward) | Iron (crystallizes) | 4-8 km thick |
| IMIC | Anisotropy boundary | Seismic waves | Fossil record of formation | Transition zone |
| Core | Superionic FeH | Hydrogen (slowly) | Iron lattice (stays) | Geological timescale |
| Tropopause | Cold trap | IR, visible light | Water vapor | Mode-dependent |
| W-Layer | Radiative heating | Longwave radiation | Entrainment (suppressed) | Newly resolved |
| Ozone | UV absorption | UV-A, visible | UV-C (total), UV-B (partial) | Wavelength-dependent |
| Stratopause | Temperature inversion | Gravity waves | Thermal structure | Cooling, dropping |
| Magnetopause | Pressure balance | Neutral particles | Charged particles | Storm-dependent |
| Heliosphere | Solar wind boundary | Neutrals, cosmic rays (partial) | Solar wind (stops) | Breathing with cycle |
| Kuiper Belt | Gravitational influence | Orbital clustering signal | Planet Nine (invisible) | Null-space detection |
| Sputnik Planitia | Ice over liquid N₂ | Liquid through cracks | Solid glacier | Active transport |

Every single one has:
- **Selective permeability**: some signals pass, some are blocked
- **Mode dependence**: different signal types see different barrier heights
- **Temporal variation**: the membrane breathes — seasons, solar cycles, geological epochs
- **Transition zones**: not walls but gradients, mushy zones, gradual thickenings
- **Memory**: the membrane retains information about what has passed through it

This is not metaphor. This is the same physics. Anderson localization in disordered media. The W parameter changes. The dimensionality changes. The signal type changes. But the *structure* is identical: selective permeability across a boundary, with propagation for some modes and localization for others.
